Basketball Shooting Profiles

Developing weekly and end-of-season shooting dashboards to help coaching staff evaluate team and player shooting tendencies.

Tools: R (Packages used: tidyverse, plotly)
Methods: Data Visualization, Basketball Analytics, Film Analysis
Domain: College Basketball

Project Summary

This project transformed shot-location data collected from practice and game film into interactive dashboards for a collegiate basketball coaching staff. Weekly editions provided visual summaries of each player's recent shooting tendencies, while the end-of-season dashboard offered a complete view of team and individual performance across defined scoring zones.

The dashboards were designed to make shooting data easier to interpret and apply during player development, practice planning, and broader offensive evaluation.

Project Overview

Traditional box-score statistics summarize whether shots were made, but they often provide limited context about where attempts occurred or how shooting behavior changed over time. This project organized individual shot attempts into meaningful scoring zones and presented the results through court-based visualizations.

Throughout the season, the coaching staff received updated shooting charts that combined practice and game-film observations. Each report provided an accessible visual outlook on how frequently and efficiently players shot from different areas of the court.

Research Question

How can practice and game-film data be transformed into clear, actionable shooting profiles that help coaches evaluate team and individual scoring tendencies?

Data

The dataset combined manually recorded shooting information from two primary sources:

  • Practice data: Shot attempts documented during team practices and shooting activities.
  • Game-film data: Shot attempts identified and coded through film review.

Each observation included information needed to identify the shooter, shot outcome, and general court location. Because the available film technology did not provide precise spatial tracking, the exact coordinates of each shot attempt could not be determined reliably. Shot locations were therefore recorded at the scoring-zone level rather than as exact points on the court.

To protect player privacy on the public portfolio, individual players are identified by jersey number rather than by name.

Methodology

The analysis pipeline converted raw shot records into team and player shooting profiles through the following process:

  1. Combined practice and game-film observations into a standardized dataset.
  2. Cleaned player identifiers, shot outcomes, and court coordinates.
  3. Assigned each shot to a predefined scoring zone.
  4. Calculated makes, attempts, shooting percentage, and shot distribution by zone.
  5. Created separate summaries for the full team and each individual player.
  6. Built an interactive dashboard that allows users to move between team and player profiles through a dropdown menu.

The visual design used multiple court views to present complementary perspectives on shooting performance, including efficiency and shot volume across the defined scoring zones.

Due to the limitations of the available game film, shot attempts were assigned to predefined scoring zones based on their visible location rather than mapped to exact court coordinates. This approach supported consistent zone-level summaries, but it limited the analysis of more granular spatial patters within each zone.

Weekly Coaching Reports

During the season, updated shooting charts were produced for the coaching staff on a weekly basis. These reports provided a current visual snapshot of each player's shooting profile and made it easier to identify changes in shot selection or efficiency.

Because the reports combined practice and game data, they offered a broader view of shooting behavior than either source could provide independently.

End-of-Season Dashboard

The end-of-season dashboard consolidates the full season of available shooting data. The default team view summarizes how the roster performed from each scoring zone, while the dropdown menu allows the user to select individual player profiles.

This structure allows coaches and analysts to compare team-wide tendencies with the shot distribution and efficiency of individual players.

Use the dropdown menu in the dashboard to move between the full-team profile and individual player profiles.

Results

The completed dashboard provides two levels of analysis:

Team Profile

The team view summarizes shooting volume and efficiency across every scoring zone, providing an overview of the offense's strongest and least efficient areas.

Player Profiles

Individual dashboards show where each player generated attempts, how efficiently those attempts were converted, and how each player's profile differed from the team as a whole.

Coaching Application

The dashboards translated detailed film and practice records into a format that could be reviewed quickly by the coaching staff.

Key Findings

  • Combining practice and game-film data created a more complete view of shooting behavior than relying on game statistics alone.
  • Zone-based summaries made differences in player shot selection and efficiency easier to identify.
  • Weekly reporting supported ongoing evaluation rather than limiting the analysis to a single end-of-season review.
  • The interactive dashboard allowed one reporting tool to serve both team-level and individual-player analysis.

Limitations

The primary limitation of the project was the precision of the available shot-location data. The film technology available to the program did not provide player-tracking data or a reliable method for identifying the exact coordinates of each attempt. As a result, shots were classified into broader scoring zones based on film review.

The dashboard should therefore be interpreted as a zone-level summary of shooting tendencies rather than a precise spatial model of where every attempt occurred. Shots near the boundary between two zones were also subject to some judgement during the coding process.

Access to optical tracking data, higher-resolution camera angles, or a standardized court-coordinate recording system would allow future versions of the analysis to examine more detailed spatial patterns.

Reflection

This project reinforced the importance of designing analytics for the people who will use them. The primary goal was not simply to produce detailed statistical output, but to create a visual tool that coaches could interpret quickly and apply within an existing basketball workflow.

It also required maintaining a repeatable data-processing and reporting pipeline throughout the season. Automating the cleaning, aggregation, and chart-building steps made it possible to produce consistent weekly updates as new practice and game data became available.

Future development could include comparisons between practice and game performance, time-based trend analysis, expected-points calculations, and filters for shot type, lineup, or game situation.
